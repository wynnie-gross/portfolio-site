### Project 1
- Title: Quantifying and Visualizing Urban Heat with Compounding Vulnerabilities to Support Community De-paving Initiatives in Portland, Oregon
- Description: Urban heat is a pressing concern in Portland, Oregon as climate change induced heat waves increase. Cities experience higher temperatures due to the urban heat island effect (UHI), and environmental injustice and disenfranchisement in minority communities expose low-income and Black, Indigenous, and People of Color (BIPOC) residents to more extreme and debilitating heat events. Our team identified Portland’s communities on the frontlines of urban heat impacts by overlapping environmental and social vulnerabilities using NASA Earth observations. We partnered with Depave, a Portland-based nonprofit that works alongside communities to replace pavement with greenspace in historically disenfranchised areas. Using Landsat 8 Thermal Infrared Sensor (TIRS) imagery, we mapped Land Surface Temperature (LST) and developed a heat-specific Social Vulnerability Index (SVI) through a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to identify Portland’s communities with the highest potential heat vulnerability. Then, we calculated the temperature change of depaving in six case studies to quantify Depave's efforts in heat mitigation and environmental justice. Our analysis demonstrated that, throughout Portland, there are frontline communities experiencing high potential social vulnerability to extreme temperatures due to environmental injustices and over-pavement. Finally, Depave’s impact on urban heat is observable and quantifiable using remote-sensing data and tools, with an average of 1ºF LST decrease across the six case studies. We illustrated the significance of local urban heat mitigation efforts and propose next steps for conducting inclusive and intentional research that highlights the lived experiences and resilience of frontline communities.
- Type: GIS
- Association: NASA DEVELOP

### Project 2
- Title: Assessing Environmental and Socioeconomic Factors of Urban Flood Vulnerability in Kansas City, Kansas
- Description: Pluvial flooding, over-saturated ground, and poor drainage systems disproportionately impact historically disinvested neighborhoods during extreme rainfall events independently of overflowing water bodies. These communities are impacted by physical and socioeconomic factors that make them vulnerable to flooding events, such as high concentration of impervious landcover, high precipitation rates, and a combined sewer system framework. Despite known vulnerability to environmental hazards, there is a lack of data supporting the potential pluvial street-level flooding events. The DEVELOP team investigated flooding events from June 2010 through June 2021 in Google Earth Engine (GEE) using NASA Earth observation products from the Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) mission. Alongside the satellite imagery and ancillary datasets, the Natural Capital Project’s Integrated Valuation of Ecosystem Services and Tradeoffs (InVEST) Urban Flood Risk Mitigation model was utilized to generate outputs of runoff retention and potential economic damage for risk mapping of Kansas City, Kansas to aid in identifying areas where future intervention is necessary. Then a cloudburst blue spot model produced spatially-explicit outputs of how pluvial flooding would accumulate across the surface elevation gradients. These resulting maps identify the most vulnerable neighborhoods throughout Kansas City, alongside potential economic damage from flooding. The resulting methodology and end products provide partners from Groundwork USA and Groundwork Northeast Revitalization Group (Groundwork NRG) with a detailed analysis of urban flood risk throughout Wyandotte County, Kansas, and streamline the provision of neighborhood-scale vulnerability analysis to Groundwork USA’s Climate Safe Neighborhoods project.
- Type: GIS
- Association: NASA DEVELOP

### Project 3
- Title: Comparing Youth Perceptions of Urban Change in Post-Industrial and Post-Colonial Cities: A Case Study Comparison of Worcester, Massachusetts and Rabat, Morocco
- Description: Stakeholders in cities across the globe are engaged in conversations about gentrification, colonial legacies, development, attracting global capital, and social justice. This study examines youth perceptions of urban change in two different geographic and economic contexts: one post-industrial Global North and one post-colonial Global South. Exploring urban geography through the youth lens offers valuable insight into urban identity, senses of belonging, community relationships, definitions of authenticity, and attitudes towards development. This research utilizes a case study comparison of Worcester, Massachusetts and Rabat, Morocco. Both cities are growing in population and face pressures to provide improved amenities to residents. Worcester is a mid-sized American city, shaped by its history as a manufacturing center. Rabat, occupied by the French Protectorate from 1912 to 1956, has colonial legacies embedded in its very footprint. Although Worcester and Rabat are different, they are representative of two major themes in urban transitions, the post-industrial city, and the post-colonial city. This study includes semi-structured interviews and participatory mapping for youth between the ages of 18 and 25. This research found that youth in both Worcester and Rabat are generally pleased with urban change in their cities. However, Moroccan youth fear infringement on old ‘authentic’ spaces and cultural heritage, whereas American youth spoke about avoiding gentrification processes. Many cities, influenced by globalization and pressures to become ‘modern,’ are at a crossroads between efforts to attract capital or adequately preserve their cultures and identities. Youth provide an avenue to better understand cities in a rapidly changing world.

- Association: Clark University

### Project 4
- Title: Mapping the Expansion of the Balama Graphite Mine in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique
- Description: This study focuses on the impact of expanding of graphite mining operations in the Cabo Delgado Province of Mozambique. Specifically, we mapped the expansion of the Balama Graphite Operation over time, from 2015 to 2024. This mine is managed by Syrah Resources, an Australian graphite and battery anode production company. This mine, located in the southern portion of Cabo Delgado, has a 110 million metric tons (Mt) Graphite Ore Reserve and a projected life of 50 years (Syrah Resources, 2023). It is the biggest graphite mine in the Cabo Delgado Province. There have been reports of violence in and around communities by the Balama Graphite Mine and in Cabo Delgado broadly, raising human rights concerns. Additionally mining has other impacts on health and livelihoods of people in the region. GIS and remote sensing can be used to assess the impacts of mining on the natural environment and lives of communities (Warner et al., 2019). For this study, we used high-resolution hyper-spectral satellite data from Planet to map the expansion of the Balama Graphite Mine over time. We used object-based classification methods, specifically segmentation, to extract mine segments based on unique spectral characteristics. In addition to mine classification and a time series analysis, we also explored Air Quality over time in Southern Cabo Delgado to assess the impact of mine expansion on health of residents.
- Type: GIS
- Association: Oxfam International

### Project 5
- Title: Identifying Lack of Ecosystem Services in Mitigating Urban Heat Island Effect in Córdoba, Argentina
- Description: The primary objective of this study is to model urban heat mitigation in Córdoba, Argentina, using the InVEST Urban Cooling model. Our central aim is to model and analyze the effectiveness of urban ecosystem services in mitigating heat and identify areas with distinctively insufficient cooling abilities. By assessing the correlation between ecosystem services and social vulnerability, we intend to shed light on the interconnected dynamics of urban heat mitigation and demographic factors in Córdoba. This study poses three key research questions: How do urban ecosystem services contribute to heat mitigation in Córdoba? Where are the areas with a notable lack of ecosystem services and cooling ability in Córdoba? What is the relationship between social vulnerability and urban heat mitigation in Córdoba?

### Project 6
- Title: Analyzing Access to Green Space in Chicago, Illinois: An Environmental Justice Approach
- Description: Urban green space is an essential public good that should be afforded to all city residents. This project examines the ways in which access to green space in Chicago is unequal spatially, socioeconomically, and racially, and how historical legacies are manifest in modern geography. Utilizing geographic information systems (GIS), we analyze the spatial relationships between sociodemographic variables, specifically race and income, and access to green space. To do so, we employed ArcGIS Pro’s Network Analyst to understand different sociodemographic groups’ access to green space within a short walking distance. In our results, we identified a spatial relationship between a lack of access to green space and marginalized identities, specifically low income populations and Black populations.
